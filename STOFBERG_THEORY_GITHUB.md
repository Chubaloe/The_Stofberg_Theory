## Abstract

The Stofberg Theory asks whether a universe containing only two kinds of fundamental magnetic monopoles, North and South, might produce the variety of structures and phenomena we observe. Each monopole is hypothesized to have no intrinsic rest mass and to move continuously at the speed of light. Opposite polarities attract, like polarities repel, and interactions change the direction of monopole motion through a distance-dependent and angle-dependent rule.

The hypothesis distinguishes two possible forms of collective organization: stable chaotic configurations associated with matter, and coordinated flows associated with magnetic fields. From these starting points, it explores possible accounts of particle structure, light, electricity, mass, inertia, gravity, time dilation, black holes, cosmic structure, and a cyclic universe. It proposes that black holes could be extreme organized magnetic systems and that the loss of stability of a universe-sized system could produce a transition associated with a Big Bang.

These ideas are **research questions**, not established consequences of the assumptions. In particular, the monopole composition of known particles is **not specified in advance**. Determining whether stable configurations exist, and whether any correspond to observed particles, is a central aim of future investigation.

# Chapter 1: Fundamental Assumptions

## 1.1 One Fundamental Constituent

The Stofberg Theory proposes that all physical phenomena emerge from a single category of fundamental entity: magnetic monopoles. Matter, radiation, magnetic structures and larger cosmic systems are proposed to consist of these same constituents, organized in different ways.

This is a simplifying hypothesis rather than an experimental finding. The existence and properties of the proposed monopoles must be tested.

## 1.2 Two Polarities

There are two proposed types of monopole, designated **North** and **South**. Matching polarities repel; opposite polarities attract. The model explores whether complex behavior can emerge from this minimal distinction without adding a separate fundamental constituent for each observed particle or interaction.

## 1.3 No Intrinsic Rest Mass

Each fundamental monopole is assumed to have no intrinsic rest mass. The mass measured for larger systems is proposed to emerge from their energy, organization and collective motion. This requires a consistent relativistic definition of energy and momentum rather than simply equating mass with a count of constituents.

## 1.4 Continuous Motion at the Speed of Light

Every monopole is assumed to move continuously at the speed of light, **c**. Interactions change its direction, not the magnitude of its speed. The trajectories may be straight in the absence of influence or continuously curved in interacting systems.

A mathematical theory must define the reference-frame meaning of this assumption and demonstrate its compatibility with relativity.

## 1.5 Interactions as Directional Redirection

The interaction between monopoles is proposed to depend on polarity, separation and relative geometry. Like-polarity repulsion and opposite-polarity attraction would alter trajectories. An inverse-square distance dependence and a sine-based angular dependence are proposed as starting assumptions.

The exact angle and interaction equation have not yet been finalized; they are subjects for research.

## 1.6 Organization Produces Different Phenomena

The hypothesis distinguishes coordinated, organized motion from stable but chaotic motion. Organized flows are proposed to underlie magnetic fields, while stable chaotic configurations are proposed to underlie matter. More complex phenomena might emerge from interactions among those systems.

## 1.7 The Fundamental Research Question

**Can two polarities of continuously moving, massless monopoles, governed by one consistent interaction law, spontaneously produce stable structures that reproduce known physical phenomena?**

The question remains open. The framework must be judged by its mathematics and testable predictions.

# Chapter 2: The Mechanics of Monopole Motion

## 2.1 Continuous Motion

Under the proposed model, a monopole never comes to rest. Its speed remains **c**, while interactions redirect its velocity vector. The resulting path can be curved or complex even though the speed is constant.

## 2.2 Directional Redirection

The fundamental effect of an interaction is a change in the direction of motion. A complete mathematical formulation must define how a requested directional change is calculated, how simultaneous influences combine, and how the condition of fixed speed is maintained.

## 2.3 Attraction and Repulsion

North-North and South-South interactions are assumed to be repulsive; North-South interactions are assumed to be attractive. These labels describe the proposed tendency to redirect paths, not a demonstrated Newtonian force law for the hypothesized entities.

## 2.4 Inverse-Square Dependence

The theory proposes that the magnitude of directional influence decreases with the square of the separation between interacting monopoles. In schematic terms, the strength is proportional to **1/r²**, where **r** is separation.

This is a modeling assumption. Its dimensions, normalization, treatment of extremely small separations and compatibility with conservation laws remain unspecified.

## 2.5 Angular Dependence

A further proposed factor depends on the sine of a geometrical angle. The idea is that the ability of one monopole to redirect another varies with their relative trajectories or orientation.

An angular factor must be defined precisely before simulations can be compared reliably. In particular, a sine factor can vanish in some alignments; the physical consequences must be worked out rather than presumed.

## 2.6 Which Angle?

At least two candidate definitions have been considered:

- **Model A:** The angle between the velocity directions of the interacting monopoles.
- **Model B:** The angle between a monopole's velocity direction and the line connecting the two monopoles.

These are not equivalent. Their predictions, symmetries and conservation properties should be evaluated separately. Neither interpretation should be described as confirmed.

## 2.7 Collective Interactions

A monopole may be influenced by many neighbors at once. The overall change in direction would depend on the combined contributions of those interactions. The formulation must specify whether influences are summed as vectors, projected perpendicular to velocity, or combined by another mathematically consistent method.

## 2.8 Stability of Motion

The central question is whether these local interactions naturally create persistent collective arrangements. Temporary proximity, constrained motion or attractive encounters are not by themselves evidence of a stable structure.

A genuine stable solution must survive appropriate perturbations without externally imposed confinement.

## 2.9 The Mathematical Challenge

The proposed mechanics must ultimately provide unambiguous equations of motion, well-defined parameters and initial conditions, numerical stability, conservation laws where applicable, and compatibility with measured physical behavior. Until then, conclusions about matter and fields remain conditional.

# Chapter 3: Two Forms of Organization

## 3.1 One Constituent, Different Structures

The Stofberg Theory proposes that the same North and South monopoles can participate in different forms of collective organization. The distinction between matter and magnetic fields would arise from their motion rather than from separate fundamental substances.

## 3.2 Emergent Organization

When monopoles interact, local changes in their trajectories may create larger patterns. Whether such patterns are stable depends on the collective dynamics, not simply on whether a visual arrangement resembles a familiar object.

## 3.3 Organized Magnetic Motion

The theory associates magnetic fields with coordinated monopole flows. These may involve curved, circulating paths and opposing contributions from North and South monopoles. The proposed organization could maintain a recognizable geometry even as its constituents continue moving.

## 3.4 Collective Stability

A monopole deviating from an organized path could experience combined influences from its neighbors. If those influences redirect it back toward the prevailing flow, the system may exhibit self-correcting behavior. This restoring behavior must be derived and tested.

## 3.5 Size and Stability

The hypothesis proposes that large organized systems could be more resistant to disruption because more monopoles contribute to their collective organization. Increasing size does not automatically imply increased stability; that relationship must be established.

## 3.6 Chaotic Motion

Matter is proposed to consist of stable configurations whose individual monopole trajectories are complex and continually changing. The motion may be chaotic without the overall structure necessarily dissolving.

## 3.7 Stability Without Fixed Trajectories

A structure could preserve its overall size, configuration or externally measurable properties while its constituent trajectories change. What matters is the persistence of the collective pattern, not a set of fixed orbits.

## 3.8 Replacement of Individual Monopoles

The theory considers whether a stable configuration might exchange monopoles with its surroundings. An incoming monopole could, under suitable conditions, replace another that leaves while preserving the larger pattern. This remains unverified.

## 3.9 Matter and Fields as Organizational States

Matter-like chaotic configurations and field-like organized flows are proposed to be two possible states of the same underlying constituents. Their different responses to interactions could explain why they appear as different phenomena.

## 3.10 Transitions Between States

The hypothesis allows for transitions. A stable chaotic configuration might be disrupted, and its constituents might be incorporated into an organized flow. Conversely, disturbances in organized motion might create conditions in which new chaotic configurations form.

## 3.11 Collective Properties

Observable properties would depend on the collective behavior of a structure, not necessarily on an individual monopole's intrinsic characteristics. Whether mass, charge, spin or other measured quantities can emerge in this way must be shown quantitatively.

## 3.12 The Central Challenge

**Can one set of interaction equations produce at least two robust, distinguishable forms of self-sustaining organization, without inserting their geometries or stability by hand?**

## 3.13 Interactions Between Organized Structures

Two organized structures may interact through the directional influence of their constituent monopoles. They might deform, change orientation, remain distinct, or merge into a combined flow. A sufficiently strong organized structure might disrupt nearby matter-like configurations and incorporate their monopoles.

These are proposed outcomes, not demonstrated ones. The conditions separating deformation, merging, disruption and separation must be identified by calculation.

## 3.14 Growth of Organized Structures

Merging and absorption could allow organized structures to grow. The hypothesis extends this idea to increasingly large magnetic systems, potentially culminating in an extreme organized structure associated with a black hole or a larger cosmological state.

# Chapter 4: The Formation of Matter

## 4.1 Matter as an Emergent Structure

The Stofberg Theory proposes that matter emerges from dynamically stable configurations of magnetic monopoles. These monopoles are not stationary. Each travels at **c** while its direction is influenced by surrounding monopoles.

Through attraction, repulsion and continuous directional changes, groups of monopoles may become trapped in complex patterns of motion. Although individual trajectories may appear chaotic, an overall configuration could remain stable.

## 4.2 The Formation of Stable Structures

Under suitable conditions, the combined interactions of several monopoles may prevent them from escaping a shared region. Such a configuration could form a dynamically stable structure without permanent bonds.

**The first research objective is to determine whether stable chaotic monopole structures can exist at all.**

## 4.3 Stability Through Collective Interaction

Every monopole within a structure contributes to the motion of others. If one begins moving outward, the combined effects of nearby constituents might redirect it inward. Stable confinement, if it exists, would emerge from the system's dynamics and need not consist of simple circular orbits.

## 4.4 Replacement of Constituents

The theory explores whether an incoming monopole could replace one that exits an existing structure without destroying its identity. In that case, matter would be a persistent organization of motion rather than a permanent set of individual constituents.

Whether this exchange can occur is an open question.

## 4.5 The Emergence of Different Particles

Different stable configurations might have different properties depending on the number of monopoles, their polarities, spatial distribution, internal trajectories and collective interactions. These properties must emerge from the model instead of being defined merely to match known particles.

## 4.6 Identifying Known Particles

**The Stofberg Theory does not currently establish how many monopoles form an electron, quark, proton, neutron or any other known particle.**

Those configurations must be discovered through mathematical analysis and simulations, not assigned in advance. If stable structures are found, their calculated properties should be compared with measured particle properties, including mass, electric charge, spin, stability and interactions.

A proposed configuration should be identified with a known particle only if it reproduces the relevant observations.

## 4.7 Matter and Magnetic Fields

Matter is associated with stable chaotic organization; magnetic fields are associated with stable coordinated flow. Both would consist of monopoles, so transitions between organizational states may be possible.

A sufficiently strong organized structure might disrupt matter and incorporate its monopoles. Conversely, the disruption of organized flow might make new chaotic structures possible.

## 4.8 Central Research Questions

- What is the smallest stable configuration, if any?
- Which numbers and polarities permit stability?
- Can constituents be exchanged while a structure persists?
- What measurable properties arise from different configurations?
- Can any configuration reproduce a known particle?
- Under what conditions do structures form, transform or disintegrate?

## 4.9 The Foundation of Matter

**The central hypothesis is that matter consists of stable, dynamically maintained organizations of magnetic monopoles.** Their exact configurations and relation to known particles are research outcomes, not premises.

# Chapter 5: Magnetic Fields

## 5.1 Magnetic Fields as Organized Motion

The Stofberg Theory proposes that magnetic fields are physical structures formed by the organized movement of monopoles. In this framework, the organized motion is the field itself.

## 5.2 Continuous Magnetic Flow

Every monopole moves at **c**. Within an organized magnetic structure, their paths are proposed to form stable, continuous trajectories. North and South monopoles may travel in opposing directions through parts of the same system, potentially forming closed circulating paths.

## 5.3 Magnetic Fields Without Matter

The theory proposes that sufficiently stable organized magnetic structures may persist without an ordinary material source. Their existence would depend on collective monopole interactions rather than a permanent material core.

This possibility has not been demonstrated.

## 5.4 Stability Through Collective Interaction

Each monopole contributes to the directional influences experienced by its neighbors. Coordinated flow could arise when these influences continually redirect the constituents along self-maintaining paths.

## 5.5 Restoring Forces

A displaced monopole experiences altered interactions because its position and orientation have changed. Under suitable conditions, the combined influences might redirect it toward its previous organized path.

A restoring force does not automatically follow from the proposed angular rule. It must be derived.

## 5.6 Stability and Structural Size

The theory proposes that larger organized structures might become more resistant to disturbance as more constituents participate in maintaining their motion. Whether the relation between size and stability is positive, negative or conditional requires investigation.

## 5.7 Interactions Between Magnetic Structures

Separate organized structures may influence each other's constituent trajectories. Their interactions depend on position, polarity, trajectory and collective organization.

## 5.8 Deformation

Approaching structures could bend, stretch, compress or reorient one another's organized paths. Such changes would not necessarily destroy either structure if their collective motion remains stable.

## 5.9 Interaction Without Merging

Some encounters might produce redirection or deformation while leaving both structures distinct. The model must identify when separation, merging or disruption occurs.

## 5.10 Structural Adaptation

Because the structures consist of moving monopoles rather than rigid components, their geometry might change continuously in response to external influences.

## 5.11 Merging of Magnetic Structures

Under suitable conditions, two organized flows might reorganize into a single larger coordinated system. The resulting structure would contain constituents from both original systems.

## 5.12 Absorption of Matter

An organized magnetic structure might disrupt nearby chaotic matter-like configurations. Monopoles released from those configurations could then join the larger organized flow.

## 5.13 Growth Through Absorption

The incorporation of monopoles from matter or smaller organized structures could increase the size or constituent count of an organized field. Not every encounter must result in absorption.

## 5.14 Resistance to Monopole Removal

The theory proposes that in sufficiently large structures, collective interactions might continually redirect incorporated monopoles toward established trajectories. Their permanent removal could become increasingly difficult. Effective irreversibility is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated property.

## 5.15 Long-Term Growth

Repeated merging and absorption could increase the fraction of monopoles participating in organized motion. In an extreme scenario, very large organized magnetic systems might dominate their surroundings.

## 5.16 Stability Versus Critical Instability

A structure may be resistant to small disturbances yet vulnerable to a global failure of its circulation. The theory proposes that extreme growth could constrain internal paths until the organized motion becomes impossible to sustain.

## 5.17 Central Research Challenge

Can the interaction rules spontaneously produce organized flows that persist, resist disturbance, deform, merge, absorb matter and eventually encounter a critical limit?

## 5.18 Black Holes as Organized Magnetic Structures

**The Stofberg Theory proposes that black holes are extreme organized magnetic structures.** Instead of a solid material core, they would consist of coordinated monopole circulation, potentially including a central passage. They might absorb matter, merge and grow while becoming highly resistant to ordinary disruption.

This is an alternative proposal about their physical nature, not an established account of astrophysical black holes. It must explain observed event horizons, gravitational effects, black hole images and merger signals. Their proposed internal structure is developed further in Chapter 8.

# Chapter 6: Light, Electricity and Energy

## 6.1 Light as a Propagating Disturbance

The Stofberg Theory proposes that light is a disturbance propagating through organized magnetic monopole structures. When one monopole deviates from its established trajectory, its interactions may cause neighboring monopoles to change direction, spreading a pattern through the field.

This treats light as a collective phenomenon rather than an independently fundamental constituent.

## 6.2 A Chain Reaction of Motion

The disturbance could pass from one group of monopoles to another without the same constituents accompanying it all the way from source to observer. The proposal resembles the transmission of a pattern through a moving system.

## 6.3 Direction of Light Propagation

The extended hypothesis associates light with disturbances that can propagate across the local flow, potentially perpendicular to the monopoles' organized direction of motion. Opposite polarities could respond differently and produce a coordinated disturbance.

## 6.4 Propagation Speed

Individual monopoles are assumed to move at **c**. The hypothesis proposes that collective disturbances may also propagate at **c**, but this does not follow automatically. A wave's propagation speed must be derived from the underlying equations.

## 6.5 Light Without Transport of One Monopole

Under the proposal, the pattern of disturbance travels even while individual monopoles continue their own organized movement. This is a hypothesis about the microscopic mechanism of radiation.

## 6.6 Different Forms of Light

The theory explores whether differences in disturbance pattern and magnitude could produce different forms of electromagnetic radiation. Its original formulation considers displacement magnitude as potentially relevant to energy.

## 6.7 The Electromagnetic Spectrum

Any successful account must reproduce radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays, together with wavelength, frequency and polarization.

Established physics measures photon energy as **E = hf**, where **h** is Planck's constant and **f** is frequency. Displacement magnitude alone cannot be assumed to determine photon energy or color.

## 6.8 Light as Collective Motion

The model must derive radiation properties from its monopole dynamics, including propagation through apparently empty space, interference, diffraction, polarization and observed quantum phenomena.

## 6.9 Electricity as Monopole Motion

The extended hypothesis proposes that electrical current may involve the movement of monopoles through matter. One exploratory idea associates current with the movement of free South monopoles between atomic structures.

This is a candidate mechanism to test, **not a confirmed identification of electrons or electric charge with individual monopoles**.

## 6.10 Electric Charge

Electric charge might emerge from polarity, arrangement and collective behavior. The actual relation between monopole polarity and measured electrical charge has not been derived, and known particles' internal monopole compositions remain undetermined.

## 6.11 Electric Fields

The extended hypothesis explores whether electric fields could reflect directional monopole motion distinct from the closed organized flows associated with magnetic fields. It must reproduce experimentally established electromagnetic laws.

## 6.12 Electrical Resistance

Interactions between moving monopoles and matter-like structures might interfere with collective motion and transfer energy to the material, creating a possible microscopic account of resistance and heating.

## 6.13 Electrical Conductivity

Different internal structures might permit monopole movement more or less readily. A quantitative account must reproduce the conductivity of real materials and its dependence on temperature and structure.

## 6.14 Superconductivity

The extended hypothesis considers whether coordinated exchanges of monopoles through matter could allow resistance-free motion. This must account for observed superconducting phenomena, including the Meissner effect and critical fields, not merely zero resistance.

## 6.15 Energy and Monopole Organization

Different forms of energy are proposed to reflect motion, interactions and organization of the same underlying constituents. This claim requires a formal definition of energy, not just a qualitative description.

## 6.16 Matter and Energy Conversion

A chaotic structure might lose its organization and release monopoles into other patterns, or an organized disturbance might help form new chaotic configurations. The framework must quantitatively explain the energy accounting in such transformations.

## 6.17 Energy Conservation

A consistent model must define energy and momentum and determine whether they are conserved in isolated systems. Fixed constituent speed by itself does not specify the energy carried by a monopole.

## 6.18 Central Research Challenge

Can the same interaction law produce radiation with the measured relation **E = hf**, electric charge and current, material conductivity, superconductivity and consistent energy-momentum conservation?

# Chapter 7: Mass, Gravity and Time

## 7.1 Mass as an Emergent Property

The Stofberg Theory proposes that monopoles have no intrinsic rest mass, while larger structures may exhibit mass through their collective energy and organization.

## 7.2 The Number of Monopoles

The number of constituents might influence a structure's observed mass, but their motion, arrangement and interaction energy would also need consideration. No simple mass-counting rule has been established.

## 7.3 Massless Constituents and Massive Structures

Relativistic physics permits a system containing massless constituents to have nonzero invariant mass when its total energy and momentum are considered together. The Stofberg Theory must develop a consistent corresponding description.

## 7.4 Inertia as Collective Behavior

Inertia might emerge from the response of a structure's internal monopole motion to an external attempt to change its overall velocity.

## 7.5 Internal Motion and External Movement

A moving matter-like structure would contain monopoles whose directions continually change while the structure has some collective motion. A change in that collective motion might require corresponding changes to many internal trajectories.

## 7.6 Resistance to Changes in Motion

A successful account must reproduce measurable force, momentum and acceleration relationships. A verbal appeal to internal complexity does not yet explain inertia.

## 7.7 Gravity and Magnetic Field Curvature

The extended hypothesis proposes that gravity could emerge from interactions between monopoles inside matter and large, curved, organized magnetic flows in their surroundings.

## 7.8 The Influence of Curved Magnetic Fields

A matter-like structure encountering a curved surrounding flow might experience a directional imbalance in the interactions acting on its constituents. The model explores whether this could produce a net acceleration toward a larger astronomical body.

## 7.9 Gravity as Collective Redirection

If many internal monopoles are redirected in a coordinated manner, the collective motion of the matter structure could change. This is a proposed microscopic mechanism, not a demonstrated substitute for spacetime curvature.

## 7.10 Gravitational Strength

The extended hypothesis explores whether the geometry, density and organization of magnetic flow determine effective gravitational influence. It must reproduce measured dependence on mass and distance.

## 7.11 Universality of Gravity

Gravitational acceleration is nearly independent of the composition of a freely falling test body. A polarity-based model must reproduce this universality and account for gravity around objects with little observable magnetic field.

## 7.12 Gravity Beyond Ordinary Matter

A viable theory must also explain gravitational lensing, orbital motion, gravitational time dilation, gravitational waves and galaxy-cluster dynamics.

## 7.13 Central Gravity Challenge

Can curved monopole flows quantitatively reproduce the phenomena currently described by general relativity, including precision tests?

## 7.14 Time as Internal Change

The theory explores whether the passage of time measured by physical clocks can be understood through the rate of changes in matter-like monopole configurations.

## 7.15 Motion and Time Dilation

The extended hypothesis proposes that as a structure's overall velocity increases, its constituent trajectories may become more aligned with collective motion, reducing internal changes relative to an external observer.

## 7.16 The Speed of Light as a Limit

This internal-motion picture is offered as a possible interpretation of relativistic time dilation. It must reproduce the full Lorentz transformations and all relevant clock experiments, not merely a qualitative slowing effect.

## 7.17 Time in Strong Magnetic Fields

The theory also explores whether strong organized magnetic flows could constrain internal monopole trajectories within matter-like structures.

## 7.18 Suppression of Internal Activity

Under extreme conditions, the extended hypothesis suggests that internal changes might become strongly suppressed. This must be distinguished from ordinary magnetic effects on atomic energy levels. Established physics does not imply that magnetic-field strength universally slows all clocks.

## 7.19 Central Research Challenge

Can a single quantitative formulation explain inertial mass, gravitation, relativistic time dilation and clock behavior in strong fields without contradicting precision measurements?

# Chapter 8: Black Holes

## 8.1 Black Holes as Organized Magnetic Structures

The Stofberg Theory proposes that astrophysical black holes might be extreme self-sustaining organized structures of magnetic monopoles. Their observed behavior would have to emerge from collective monopole motion rather than being assumed from the outset.

## 8.2 A Structure Without a Solid Core

The proposed structure would have no solid material center. It would consist of monopoles traveling continuously along coordinated, curved trajectories.

## 8.3 Black Holes and Magnetic Fields

Under the hypothesis, black holes are extreme cases of the organized magnetic flows described in Chapter 5, not a separate type of fundamental substance.

## 8.4 Continuous Monopole Circulation

North and South monopoles might circulate along organized loops, potentially traveling in opposing directions through different regions. A dipole-like overall geometry is one candidate arrangement.

## 8.5 The Central Passage

The extended hypothesis proposes a central region through which monopoles circulate. Paths might curve around the outer structure and return through this passage. It is not proposed to be a solid core.

## 8.6 Maintaining the Flow

The circulation would require continuous directional redirection maintained by collective interactions. The existence and stability of the proposed central passage must be demonstrated, not assumed.

## 8.7 Collective Stability

Many interacting monopoles might produce a structure resistant to modest disturbances. Deviating constituents could potentially be redirected toward the organized circulation.

## 8.8 Resistance to Disruption

Increasing size might make individual monopoles harder to dislodge from the collective flow, although the dependence of stability on size is not known.

## 8.9 Deformation Without Destruction

The structures might change shape or orientation through interactions without losing their overall organization. Global instability remains possible even if local restoring behavior exists.

## 8.10 Matter Encountering a Black Hole

A matter-like chaotic structure approaching an intense organized flow might experience strong directional influences on its constituents.

## 8.11 Breakdown of Matter

Those interactions might destabilize the configuration, potentially disrupting progressively smaller structures. Whether this occurs and under what conditions remains unknown.

## 8.12 Incorporation Into Organized Flow

Released monopoles might join the larger coordinated circulation. In this picture, the fundamental constituents would be reorganized rather than destroyed.

## 8.13 Growth Through Absorption

Absorbing monopoles could increase the size or density of the organized structure. Energy and momentum conservation must be demonstrated.

## 8.14 Interactions Between Black Holes

Two proposed black-hole structures could influence one another through their constituent trajectories and collective organization.

## 8.15 Merging Into Larger Structures

Under suitable conditions, distinct flows might merge into a larger coordinated system. The model must reproduce observations of merging astrophysical black holes, including gravitational-wave signals.

## 8.16 Information and Structural Identity

Matter absorbed into a reorganized flow might lose its prior configuration. Whether information about the original arrangement is destroyed or encoded in the larger system remains unresolved. Information loss is not established by the proposal.

## 8.17 Long-Term Evolution

Growth through absorption and merging could produce increasingly large organized structures over long periods.

## 8.18 Constraints on Internal Motion

The extended hypothesis proposes that growth could change circulation geometry, strengthen inward directional influences and potentially narrow the central passage.

## 8.19 A Critical Limit

At some extreme configuration, the paths required for continuing circulation might become impossible to maintain, producing a system-wide instability. This is a conjecture that requires a derived dynamical threshold.

## 8.20 Connection to the Big Bang

The hypothesis extends this possible instability to an enormous, potentially universe-sized organized structure. Its breakdown might redistribute monopoles into chaotic motion, forming the basis for a proposed cosmic cycle.

**Observational requirement:** Any viable black-hole model must account for event horizons, strong gravitational fields, lensing, orbital behavior, black hole imaging and merger observations. These properties have not been derived from the Stofberg Theory.

# Chapter 9: The Big Bang

## 9.1 The Big Bang as a Transformation

The Stofberg Theory proposes that the event associated with the Big Bang might have been a large-scale transition in how already-existing monopoles were organized, rather than their creation.

## 9.2 The Universe Before the Big Bang

The extended hypothesis envisions a preceding cosmic stage in which organized magnetic systems had grown through absorption and merging, eventually producing a dominant system.

## 9.3 No Creation of Fundamental Constituents

The proposed transition would reorganize existing monopoles from coordinated circulation into less ordered motion. Their creation or destruction would not be required within this conjecture.

## 9.4 Growth of Organized Motion

Organized structures might increase their constituent count by merging with other flows or absorbing monopoles from destabilized matter.

## 9.5 Toward a Universe-Sized Structure

In an extreme scenario, one enormous organized flow could encompass a substantial portion of the available monopoles. Its resistance to local disturbances would not necessarily make it globally stable.

## 9.6 Increasing Internal Constraints

Growth might constrain the circulation geometry, potentially narrowing the central passage while more monopoles participate in the flow.

## 9.7 A Limit to Organized Circulation

At a critical point, the trajectories required to maintain coordinated motion might become unsustainable.

## 9.8 Structural Instability

A disruption in one region could alter neighboring trajectories and propagate through the structure, creating a possible large-scale loss of organization.

The existence of a narrowed passage alone does not establish this result; the instability must follow from the equations.

## 9.9 A Collective Breakdown

The proposed Big Bang mechanism is a collective failure of an enormous organized system, not an explosion of newly created monopoles.

## 9.10 From Organization to Chaos

The monopoles would continue moving and interacting at **c**, but their former collective circulation would no longer constrain them to the same organized paths.

## 9.11 Emergence of New Structures

Within the resulting chaotic environment, interactions might create new stable matter-like configurations and smaller organized magnetic structures.

## 9.12 Energy and Conservation

A viable model must calculate the energy and momentum accounting through the transition and explain the observed radiation and matter content of the early universe.

## 9.13 Expansion of the Universe

The hypothesis explores whether the large-scale redistribution of monopoles could account for observed cosmic expansion. Motion outward through a pre-existing space is not automatically equivalent to the expansion inferred in standard cosmology.

## 9.14 Expansion Without New Monopoles

Cosmic evolution could involve the redistribution of existing constituents, but the model must quantitatively explain cosmological redshifts and distances.

## 9.15 The Early Universe

Any alternative mechanism must reproduce the cosmic microwave background, light-element abundances, cosmic expansion history, galaxy formation and the distribution of large-scale structure. Modern observations support an early hot, dense phase; the proposed microscopic mechanism must account for that evidence.

## 9.16 Formation of a New Universe

After the proposed transition, chaotic matter-like configurations and organized flows might form and interact, beginning another period of evolution.

## 9.17 A Cyclic Universe

A hypothetical cycle is:

1. Formation of matter and organized fields.
2. Growth of organized structures through merging and absorption.
3. Emergence of a dominant organized system.
4. Increasing constraints on circulation.
5. Critical system-wide instability.
6. Redistribution into chaotic motion and renewed structure formation.

No stage has yet been shown to follow necessarily from the preceding stage.

## 9.18 Central Research Challenge

Can the proposed interaction law produce such a transition and yield a cosmic state consistent with the measured early universe and subsequent expansion?

# Chapter 10: The Evolution and Structure of the Universe

## 10.1 A Universe of Moving Monopoles

The theory proposes that North and South monopoles underlie all cosmic structures. Following a hypothetical large-scale breakdown, their continuing interactions might generate both chaotic matter-like systems and organized fields.

## 10.2 Development of Cosmic Structure

As structures interact, larger systems could emerge. Organized flows might grow through merging and absorption, while matter-like systems might associate into increasingly complex arrangements.

## 10.3 An Uneven Distribution

Variations in initial distribution and local organization could affect where structures develop. A quantitative model must explain the origin and growth of these variations.

## 10.4 Formation of Galaxies

The Stofberg Theory explores whether matter and organized magnetic structures together could influence galaxy formation. It has not yet derived a galaxy-formation mechanism.

## 10.5 Galactic Motion

The hypothesis explores whether large-scale monopole organization could contribute to observed stellar and gas motions. It must reproduce galactic rotation curves, gravitational lensing and cluster dynamics.

## 10.6 The Cosmic Web

The observable universe contains galaxy clusters, filaments and relatively empty regions. The model must determine whether the evolution of monopole organization can produce their measured distribution.

## 10.7 Nature of Cosmic Voids

Cosmic voids are regions with fewer galaxies than their surroundings. The theory explores whether some could be related to large organized magnetic structures that affect matter distribution.

## 10.8 Matter Depletion

An organized flow might hypothetically disrupt matter-like structures and incorporate their monopoles, reducing matter in a region. Whether this mechanism can operate on cosmic scales is unknown.

## 10.9 Testing the Void Hypothesis

Standard cosmology already explains voids through the growth of underdense regions. The proposed alternative must reproduce observed void sizes, shapes, galaxy populations and evolution, and ideally provide distinguishing predictions.

## 10.10 The Dark Matter Question

The theory explores whether interactions with large organized magnetic structures might produce some gravitational effects attributed to dark matter. This is untested. It must account for galaxy rotation, lensing, cluster observations and the cosmic microwave background.

## 10.11 The Dark Energy Question

The observed expansion of the universe is accelerating. The theory asks whether changing monopole organization could generate such a result, but it has not yet derived accelerated cosmic expansion.

## 10.12 Quantitative Predictions

Neither dark matter nor dark energy can be replaced by a verbal alternative alone. The model must produce predictions that match observations at least as well as existing descriptions.

## 10.13 The Extent of the Universe

The hypothesis raises questions about whether monopoles may exist beyond the region we can observe. The observable universe is limited by information reaching us; it is not necessarily the physical edge of all existence.

## 10.14 Boundaries Between Structures

Large organized systems might contain boundaries where flow interacts strongly with less organized surroundings. Matter approaching such a boundary might be redirected, disrupted or incorporated.

## 10.15 Regions Beyond Organized Fields

Monopoles outside large organized structures might participate in less coordinated movement and influence the surrounding systems. Such regions and boundaries must emerge from the model rather than be assumed.

## 10.16 Competition Between Matter and Fields

The cosmic balance of chaotic matter-like configurations and organized flows may change over time through formation, disruption and absorption.

## 10.17 Growth Toward a Dominant Structure

Repeated absorption and merging might eventually produce a very large organized system. Whether growth can proceed indefinitely or on relevant cosmological timescales is unknown.

## 10.18 Return to Critical Instability

If a unified organized structure formed, it might encounter the hypothetical circulation limit described in Chapter 9 and transition toward less organized motion.

## 10.19 Central Research Challenge

Can one quantitatively consistent monopole framework explain the measured cosmic web, galaxy dynamics, voids, expansion history and long-term evolution?

# Chapter 11: Predictions, Open Questions and Research Priorities

## 11.1 Current Status

The Stofberg Theory proposes two types of fundamental magnetic monopoles, no intrinsic rest mass, continuous movement at **c**, and polarity-, distance- and angle-dependent directional interactions. It explores whether all larger phenomena might emerge from those assumptions.

These proposals have not yet been mathematically or experimentally demonstrated. They should be treated as an exploratory hypothesis, not accepted results.

## 11.2 Importance of Testing

A scientific model must produce predictions that can be compared with observations and that could show the model to be wrong. The immediate task is to determine what actually follows from the proposed interaction rules.

## 11.3 Defining the Fundamental Interaction

The first research priority is to establish precise equations covering two polarities, attraction and repulsion, fixed constituent speed, an inverse-square distance factor, a proposed sine-based angle factor and the combined effects of many simultaneous interactions.

## 11.4 The Angular Interaction Rule

Two possible definitions need investigation: the angle between the velocities of interacting monopoles, or the angle between velocity and separation. These interpretations must be tested independently.

## 11.5 Mathematical Consistency

The equations must specify how directions change while speed remains fixed, how interactions combine, how energy and momentum are defined, and whether the dynamics are compatible with relativity and other established constraints.

## 11.6 First Simulation Objective

Once equations are defined, simulations should study small systems with varied initial conditions. Their purpose is to discover emergent behavior, not to recreate a predetermined particle or magnetic structure.

## 11.7 Searching for Stable Configurations

Research questions include:

- Can monopoles remain dynamically confined?
- Do stable chaotic configurations emerge?
- Do organized circulating flows emerge?
- Which systems withstand perturbations?
- How do size, polarity balance and initial conditions affect stability?
- Can constituents enter and leave while a pattern persists?

## 11.8 Avoiding Artificial Stability

Structures maintained by imposed boundaries, forced trajectories or numerical artifacts are not evidence of spontaneous self-sustaining configurations. Stability criteria and sensitivity tests must be reported.

## 11.9 Comparing Models

Different interaction laws and angular interpretations should be compared using identical tests where possible. Failed or unstable results are valuable and should be documented.

## 11.10 Identifying Matter

If stable chaotic configurations emerge, their properties should be calculated before identifying them with known particles. **The numbers and arrangements of monopoles in electrons, quarks, protons and neutrons are unknown and should not be assigned in advance.**

## 11.11 Investigating Magnetic Fields

If stable flows emerge, researchers can investigate their geometry, external influence, response to disturbances, deformation, merging and possible absorption of monopoles from other structures.

## 11.12 Testing Light, Electricity and Gravity

Advanced research should determine whether the emergent systems reproduce electromagnetic radiation, electric phenomena, inertia, gravity and precise relativistic observations.

## 11.13 Black Holes

The theory proposes extreme organized flows as possible black-hole analogues. Whether such systems exist, absorb matter, merge and reproduce observed astrophysical black-hole properties must be demonstrated.

## 11.14 Proposed Big Bang Mechanism

A cyclic cosmology would require showing that organized structures can grow, reach a critical instability, break down and yield conditions consistent with the observed early universe.

## 11.15 Distinctive Predictions

The research program should prioritize measurable predictions that distinguish the framework from established theories. Candidate areas include the stability of organized configurations, extreme-field behavior and specific astronomical effects. A suggested effect becomes a prediction only after it follows quantitatively from the equations.

## 11.16 Suggested Research Order

1. Define the monopole interaction equations.
2. Test mathematical and physical consistency.
3. Investigate small systems under varied conditions.
4. Search for naturally stable structures.
5. Calculate emergent properties.
6. Compare predictions with measured physics.
7. Investigate larger systems and cosmology only if the foundations withstand testing.

## 11.17 Collaboration and Critical Examination

Contributions from mathematicians, physicists, programmers, students and independent researchers are welcome. Valuable work includes formal derivations, numerical experiments, critical examination, alternative formulations and comparisons with observations.

Disagreement, counterexamples and negative results are essential parts of scientific investigation.

## 11.18 The Central Open Question

**Can the observed complexity of the universe emerge from two polarities of continuously moving magnetic monopoles interacting through one consistent set of fundamental rules?**

The answer is not known. The purpose of stating the Stofberg Theory is to make that question precise enough to investigate and potentially falsify.

# Research Notes and Scope

## What the theory assumes

- Two hypothesized monopole polarities: North and South.
- No intrinsic rest mass for individual monopoles.
- Continuous monopole motion at the speed of light.
- Directional redirection through polarity-dependent interactions.
- Candidate inverse-square distance dependence and sine-based angular dependence.

## What the theory does **not** yet establish

- A complete, uniquely specified interaction equation.
- Experimental evidence for the proposed monopoles.
- Robustly stable chaotic or organized solutions.
- The monopole composition of any known particle.
- A derivation of measured masses, charges, spin or particle spectra.
- A validated explanation of light, electricity, gravity or time dilation.
- A black-hole structure reproducing astrophysical observations.
- A cosmological model reproducing the early universe and cosmic expansion.

## Priority for future work

The foundational equations and stability tests take priority over claims about known particles or cosmology. If a proposed interaction cannot generate suitable stable configurations, later claims must be revised accordingly.
